> Verbatim record of the owner's instructions, reproduced as received, with what he had been shown between them.
> In summary: the net is marked one corner at a time — a single instruction per corner, four taps, each moving on
> by itself, with Back; the court is marked by fitting an outline that is already on the picture, the page opening
> zoomed out because the near corners are usually out of sight (D-047).

**The owner's question:**

is there a more intuitive way to be able to mark the nets and courts? can we think about flow and mobile flow and come up with some different options that are more intuitive than the current system?

**What the owner was told (Claude Code):** what makes the pages hard (ten points in a set order, near corners out of the picture, the net's bottom corners within a finger's width of its top ones, many controls, a run not reading the court) and four options: net only in two taps, a shape dragged to fit, tracing lines, and a suggestion to confirm.

**The owner's answers:**

court already is optional and as of right now i dont think it does anything but just build an artifact with the different options and flows or build it in figma

net and court

**What the owner was then shown:** an artifact of working phone mock-ups on a frame of a beach recording — five ways to mark the net and three to mark the court — each timing the attempt and scoring it against marks placed by hand.

**The owner's answers, on trying them:**

i like the single command and you tap each one and it auto goes and there should be a back and but each screen says tap top left then right the bottom but its a simple screen for the net. for the court im not sure yet

four taps, and add the same style for the court

i like the fit the court shape the most honestly, like it starts a little more zoomed out because most of the time the close corners will be occluded
